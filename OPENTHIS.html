The person is never separated from their context. Temporal, sociocultural, environmental and task elements continuously shape — and are shaped by — occupational performance, experience and participation.

Top-Down Approach

The OTIPM always starts with the person's occupational world — never with impairment. Understanding roles and routines comes before performance factors.

1
Occupational Roles — Who is this person? What roles do they hold?
2
Routines — What does a typical day look like?
3
Occupations — What specific activities hold meaning and priority?
4
Performance Factors — Only then: what underlies or limits performance?

Evaluation, intervention and re-evaluation repeat in a continuous cycle — each loop refining goals and strategies as performance changes.

Under Intervention · 4 Frames of Reference
Adaptive
Modify the task, environment or tools to support current performance.
Compensatory
Use alternative strategies or equipment to enable participation now.
Acquisitional
Learn new skills through practice in real, meaningful occupational contexts.
Rehabilitative
Restore underlying skills and body functions through occupation-based activity.

06 · Occupational Profile
The Person Behind the Patient

Pre-Morbid

Fully independent in all bADLs and iADLs. Active social life, family involvement, community participation.

Now

Unable to sit up, stand, or move without assistance. Dependent in all self-care. At odds with the independent man he had been.

Occupational Shift

Early in the admission he expressed distress at being in hospital and unable to care for himself. Over months, the ward has become normalised

His Priorities

Return to independent mobility, independence in personal care. Going home and resumed participation in hobbies
